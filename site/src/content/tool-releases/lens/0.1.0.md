The first version of lens: the numbers about a picture that looking at it doesn't give, and about a sound that an agent can't hear at all.

## Made for coding agents

AI coding agents can look at a picture but not measure it, and they can't listen at all. So they write a throwaway command for each number. In the agent transcripts of one machine, 3,022 shell commands worked on images or sounds, and reading an image's size with Python's PIL alone came up 530 times. lens gives those numbers in one command, with an answer of a known size.

## Images

- **The exact size**, with its orientation and its aspect ratio when it is a common one (`16:9`, `2:3`), the kind of color the file stores, and the frames of an animated GIF or PNG.
- **Real transparency or a painted checkerboard.** lens says how much of the picture is clear, how much is partly clear (soft edges, a glow, a shadow), and whether the border is clear, which means a real transparent background, as around a cut-out. Image generators sometimes paint the gray checkerboard that stands for "transparent" into the pixels. lens calls that fake transparency, with the two grays and the size of the squares.
- **The drawn area** as `WxH+X+Y`, the form ImageMagick's `-crop` takes, and the command that cuts the picture to it when it is under 90% of the picture.
- **The main colors** with their share, and the number of different colors. Colors of the border are marked `(background)`.
- PNG, JPEG, GIF, WebP, BMP and TIFF, up to 16384x16384 pixels. A bigger picture is refused before it is decoded, since it would take gigabytes of memory.

```
$ lens icon.png
lens: icon.png  PNG, 512x512 square, RGBA 16-bit, 9 KB
alpha     44% clear, under 0.1% partly (soft edges, glow or shadow); the border is clear: a real transparent background
drawn     384x384+64+64, 56% of the image (the rest is clear)
colors    #8f7cff 87%, #e7e3ff 12%; 34 distinct colors
next: magick icon.png -crop 384x384+64+64 +repage icon-trim.png   cut it to the drawn area
```

## Two images

- **`lens diff before.png after.png` says what changed**: how many pixels, in how many spots, and each spot's box, the largest first.
- **One crop for both.** lens prints the same `magick -crop` command for before and after, with 16 pixels around the changes (`--pad`), so the two crops line up. When the changes are far apart, so that one crop around all of them would be mostly things that didn't change, it gives one crop per spot instead.
- **The noise of a JPEG isn't a change.** With a lossy picture, a pixel has to differ by more than 3% to count (`--fuzz`). When most changes are tiny, lens says so and suggests `--fuzz 10`.
- **`-o diff.png`** writes a picture to look at: before, after, and after with the changed pixels in red, side by side.

## Sounds

- **Length, peak and loudness.** The peak, the RMS level and the loudness in LUFS, the number that loudness targets such as -14 or -23 LUFS mean, measured as the ITU-R BS.1770 standard says, which is also what ffmpeg's `ebur128` measures. lens also says whether the sound clips.
- **Silence at the start and at the end** (below -60 dBFS, which `--silence` changes), and pauses of 250 ms or more in between.
- **Stereo**: both channels the same, one of them silent, in opposite phase (they cancel each other out when mixed to mono), or one louder than the other.
- **Tone**: pitched, noise-like or mixed, the strongest frequency with its note (`880 Hz (A5)`), and how much of the energy is low, middle and high.
- **A waveform and a spectrum in text**, 60 columns wide, so silence and fades show as they sound. `-o voice.png` writes a waveform over a spectrogram as a picture.
- **WAV, MP3 and Ogg Vorbis are read by lens itself**; Opus, FLAC, M4A and others need `ffmpeg` and `ffprobe`. A file that ends before its header says it should, or a damaged one, gets a `warning` line, and lens exits with 2. A sound of any length is measured with little memory.

## Several files

- **A folder or several files give one line per file**, the images and the sounds in tables of their own. A folder is read one level deep.

Three reviews and their re-checks went through lens before this tag, and each problem they found has a test of its own.
